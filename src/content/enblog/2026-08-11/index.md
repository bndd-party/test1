## My First Grizzly Panda Kit

I was immediately drawn to this kit’s beautiful body sculpting.  
The pose and expression are adorable, too.  

That combination of beauty and cuteness was what sold me on the kit.  

This was my first Grizzly Panda kit. I bought it six years ago—and then left it on the shelf for six years. 😅  

I finally started building it when I decided to attend a garage kit exhibition. It was great to get back into a kit with this much energy.  

After six years, she is finally finished. A very special one for me.

## Kit Information

Dealer: Grizzly Panda  
Parts: 41  

The kit is called Usagino Mimomo & Miki, but it does not include two figures. They are two color variations:  

Mimomo: Pink hair + Alice-style maid outfit  
Miki: Blonde hair + black maid outfit  

I went with my own color scheme.

## Paint Testing

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/color1.avif" alt="Paint color swatches" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

I wanted a chocolate mint color scheme! 🌿🍫  
First, I tested some colors.  

From left: Gaia Mechatro WeGo Usumidori, Miku Green, Finishers Cerulean Blue, Miku Hair Shadow, and Mr. Color Cerulean Blue.  

Although Miku Green is called “Green,” it looks more like a light blue to me.  
Usumidori felt a little too pale, so I chose Miku Green as the base.  

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/color2.avif" alt="Green and mint paint color swatches" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Next, I tested some greener mint colors.  

From left: Volks Wakakusa Green, Miku Body Shadow, and Finishers Peppermint.  

I also included Mr. Color Cerulean Blue, the bluest color from the first test that looked the most green.  
For a stronger mint tone, I chose Finishers Peppermint.  

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/color3.avif" alt="Chocolate-colored paint swatches" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

For the chocolate color, I tested Mr. Color Oarai Brown, Hull Red, and Finishers Chocolate.  

I had never tried Finishers Chocolate before, and it really does look like chocolate!  
I decided to use all three, with Oarai Brown as the base.

## Painting

### 1. Legs

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/leg1.avif" alt="Skin tone painted on the legs" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/leg2.avif" alt="Stockings painted on the legs" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

I started with white primer, followed by a slightly heavier coat of Mr. Color Surfacerless Flesh Pink.  
For the stockings, I used Mr. Color Stocking Brown with a few drops of Gold Pearl Base.  

I finished them with a light mist of Moonstone Pearl and Topaz Gold for a soft, slightly satin-like look.

### 3. Hair

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/kami1.avif" alt="Testing the inner hair color" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

I first painted the inner hair color and checked how much of it would be visible.  
I used this color as the endpoint of the hair gradient, then painted the front and back sections to match.  

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/kami2.avif" alt="Hair color and gradient" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

For the base, I used Volks Wakakusa Green.  
I added Volks Sakura Pink from the top of the head, then layered Mint Green toward the tips.  
After a coat of Super Smooth Clear, the colors became a little softer and more subdued.  

---

### 4. Clothes & Accessories

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/komono.avif" alt="Bra and accessories" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

Bra:  
Miku Body Shadow as the base, with Finishers Peppermint added to the shadow areas.  

Mint ribbons:  
Finishers Cerulean Blue as the base, with Peppermint added near both ends.  

Chocolate-colored ribbons:  
Oarai Brown as the base, with Finishers Chocolate added near both ends.  

I wanted a two-tone ribbon effect, so I masked the colors separately. Unfortunately, the boundary ended up a little uneven.   
Definitely something to improve next time. 😓  

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/frill1.avif" alt="Painted frills on the legs" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

For the frills on the arms, legs, and head, I carved and polished the inner surfaces with a round file.  
However, the original sculpting was already so good that the difference was quite subtle.  
The bigger problem was the paint. The delicate frills ended up looking too flat, especially on the arms.  
The frills are definitely my biggest lesson from this build. 😵  

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/kusari.avif" alt="Ankle shackles and chains" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

I added a very subtle touch of Clear Purple and Mint to the chains around the neck and legs.  
You can see it in the close-up photos, but it is almost invisible to the naked eye. I probably could have made it a little stronger.

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/base2.avif" alt="Rabbit display base" />

## A Beautiful Sculpt Made This Easy

<figure>
  <img src="/images/blog/2026-08-11/en5.avif" alt="Finished figure, full view" />
  <figcaption></figcaption>
</figure>

This was such a fun build!  

The biggest thing I noticed was how easy the kit was to assemble and paint. The sculpt is so good that even a fairly simple paint job looks great.  

That beautiful sculpting helped me enormously throughout the process.  

It was the kind of kit that made me feel like I was doing a better job than I actually was—and that made the whole experience incredibly motivating.   

By the way, Usagino Mimomo is an original kit and is still available from Grizzly Panda’s BOOTH as of August 2026.  

If this post makes you think, “Maybe I could build one too,” I couldn't be happier.
